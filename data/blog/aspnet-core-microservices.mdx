Microservices architecture has become the backbone of enterprise applications. At First American India, I've spent the past year building and maintaining ASP.NET Core 8 microservices that serve US county-level government clients.

## Why Microservices?

Monolithic applications work well for small teams and simple domains. But when you're dealing with financial data platforms that need independent scaling, deployment, and team ownership, microservices shine.

Key benefits I've experienced firsthand:

- **Independent deployment** — Ship one service without touching others
- **Technology flexibility** — Pick the right tool per bounded context
- **Fault isolation** — A failure in one service doesn't bring down the entire platform

## Architecture Patterns

### Clean Architecture + Repository Pattern

Every service I build follows SOLID principles with a clear separation of concerns:

- **Domain layer** — Entities, value objects, domain events
- **Application layer** — Use cases, CQRS handlers, DTOs
- **Infrastructure layer** — EF Core repositories, external API clients
- **Presentation layer** — Minimal APIs or controllers with Swagger/OpenAPI

### Event-Driven Communication

For asynchronous decoupling, we use Azure Service Bus and Kafka. When a county is onboarded in Geo Tools, an event is published that NGS 2.0 consumes to configure financial policy rules — no direct coupling between services.

## Performance Wins

Through query optimization, distributed caching with Redis, and async/await multithreading, we improved backend throughput by 35%. Small changes like proper indexing and avoiding N+1 queries make a measurable difference at scale.

## Takeaways

Start with a well-defined bounded context. Invest in observability early (Application Insights is your friend). And always document your APIs with Swagger — your future self will thank you.
